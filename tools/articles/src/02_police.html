The short answer

In GTA 6, the police only know what someone told them. A crime has to be witnessed or set off an alarm before it's reported, and the icons under your stars show what they know: what you're driving, whether you're a couple, what you're wearing and more. Change what they're looking for, leave the area, and you can get away.

  • No witness, no wanted level: a crime needs a witness or an alarm to be reported, Rockstar's Rob Nelson told IGN.
  • Hollow stars: the police don't know who you are.
  • New outfit: buying and wearing one removes your Heat, Nelson says.
  • The Criminal Profile: tracks what sort of criminal you are, separately from Jason and Lucia's relationship.

How we did this: almost everything here comes from Rockstar North's Rob Nelson on the record, in IGN's full interview and Famitsu's interview (in Japanese, so we paraphrase it rather than quote it), plus Edge magazine's preview as other outlets reported it. Where something is an outlet's own reading rather than Rockstar's words, we say so.

How do you get a wanted level in GTA 6?

Someone has to see you, or an alarm has to go off. Nelson told IGN that the Wanted system needs a witness or an alarm before a crime is reported to the police. In Famitsu's interview, he says that idea comes from Red Dead Redemption 2, where you aren't wanted unless somebody saw you.

Once a crime is reported, there are a few ways the police hear about it:

  • Passers-by may call it in during a robbery (Famitsu).
  • The owner of a car you've stolen may report it (Famitsu).
  • Alarms in stores and buildings report the crime for you (IGN).

According to Famitsu, how many stars you get depends on what the crime is and how serious it is. One star isn't going to be too difficult to get away from, Nelson told IGN. If you turn on the police, though, they turn on you too (Famitsu).

What do the hollow stars mean?

Hollow white stars mean the police don't know who you are. That's Nelson's own explanation in IGN's demo: the police went to where the car was stolen, but didn't know what Jason and Lucia looked like. Preview coverage also reports up to six stars, one more than GTA 5.

What the other star colours mean is less clear. GamesRadar's guide gives its own reading, and says that's the writer's interpretation rather than Rockstar's. We'd go with Rockstar's hollow-star explanation; Rockstar hasn't explained the other colours.

What do the icons under the stars mean?

They show what the police know about you. In IGN's demo, Nelson points at them and explains that the police know what he's driving and that they're a couple. Edge's preview, as reported by other outlets, describes them as the contents of the police report. Six have been seen so far, according to GamesRadar:

About hair: Nelson told IGN that the safehouse can only cut your hair shorter or shave it off. For new styles you need a salon or barber, and each salon has its own signature styles as well as the standard ones.

Can the police see you on camera?

Yes, and you can do something about it. In Famitsu's interview, Nelson describes destroying security cameras once you're inside. He also describes disabling the security system to keep an alarm quiet, and a lock-pick called a snap gun for getting in without breaking the door.

Masks matter too. According to Famitsu, if a witness reports you wearing a mask, taking it off afterwards makes you harder to recognise.

How do you lose the cops in GTA 6?

Get out of the area. Famitsu describes Heat as the state where the police are searching for you, and once you're out of that area, the search ends, though police can still spot a vehicle they've flagged. Nelson showed IGN a few more things:

  • The map shows a hot area. Go back into it and the police are still hanging around. Hovering over it shows you still have a star there.
  • Use the back door. After a robbery in IGN's demo, the police gathered out front and Nelson left through the back.
  • Get on foot. At three stars in the city, Nelson says your best option is usually to get off the streets and into the alleys on foot, unless you have a very fast car or sports bike.
  • Use Focus. Focus helps you find things on the map, Nelson told IGN, and that includes cops. He told the YouTuber TGG (The Gaming Gorilla) that police show up when you use Focus, GamesRadar reported.
  • Walk away from the stolen car. The police head for it (Famitsu).

Nelson told TGG the team wanted to get past losing the cops instantly, giving the police a bit more memory, GamesRadar reported. Expect a chase to take more thought than in GTA 5.

What is the Criminal Profile?

It's a system that tracks what sort of criminal you are. Nelson told IGN it's different from Red Dead Redemption 2's honor system and separate from Jason and Lucia's relationship. There isn't a right or wrong way to play, he says, but consistent behaviour may affect how the world reacts to you and how the story plays out.

What counts? Doing what a mission needs, robbing places and dealing with someone who comes at you are all fair game, Nelson told IGN. It only starts to shift when you consistently go beyond what's necessary. Famitsu's example: getting out of a jewellery store fast can move you towards the better side of the scale.

TGG's preview, reported by PC Gamer, lists labels including Professional, Aggressive, Violent and Psycho. That's a previewer's account and Rockstar hasn't published the labels, so treat the list as unconfirmed.

What happens to your cash when you're Busted?

If either of you is arrested, it's Busted and a restart, as in earlier games, Nelson told IGN. The cash you're carrying is at risk when you're Wasted or Busted, Famitsu reports, and VGC's launch guide says you lose it. Bank it.

ROCKSTAR SAID

  • Hollow stars mean the police don't know who you are.
  • A new outfit removes Heat; a new hairstyle alone doesn't fool them.
  • The Criminal Profile is separate from the relationship system.
