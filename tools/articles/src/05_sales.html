The short answer

Take-Two and Rockstar say GTA 6 made $3.79 billion in day-one retail sales worldwide, by the company's own estimate through the end of 19 November, and sold 32.5 million units before release. Take-Two calls the first figure a record for entertainment in any medium. Those are the official numbers. Anything you saw before 20 November was an estimate.

  • Official: $3.79 billion and 32.5 million pre-release units, announced on 20 November.
  • GTA 5 for comparison: more than $800 million in retail sales on its first day in 2013, Take-Two said at the time.
  • Estimates: Sensor Tower's estimates, which were all over the news on launch day, came in lower.

How we did this: the official figures come from Take-Two and Rockstar's 20 November announcement, which Take-Two filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Comparisons come from Take-Two's 2013 figures and Guinness World Records. Every estimate is labelled as one.

How much money did GTA 6 make on day one?

$3.79 billion in day-one retail sales, according to Take-Two. Its footnote says that's a company estimate through the end of the day on 19 November, worldwide. Some headlines called it the first 24 hours, but Take-Two's own wording is "day one", and it doesn't give a start time or time zone, so we can't say whether it's exactly 24 hours. Take-Two says it set a new record for entertainment across any medium.

The game unlocked at midnight local time in most places, with New Zealand first, and at midnight Eastern across the US, GGRecon reports.

How many copies has GTA 6 sold?

Take-Two says GTA 6 sold 32.5 million units before release, a record across all platforms. It hasn't given a figure for copies sold since launch, and it doesn't say whether the $3.79 billion includes those pre-release sales. Earlier, in its 9 November earnings release, CEO Strauss Zelnick said pre-orders were continuing to reach unprecedented levels.

"GTA 6 made $1.84 billion." That was a real headline, but it was a Sensor Tower estimate of sales by release day, not a Take-Two figure. Take-Two's official total came in well above it.

Did more people buy it on PlayStation or Xbox?

Take-Two hasn't said. The often-quoted split of about 75% PlayStation and 25% Xbox comes from Sensor Tower's estimates, not an official number.

What happens next?

Take-Two's next earnings report will be the first to show GTA 6 in its full numbers. In its 9 November release, it kept its outlook for the financial year at $8.0 to $8.2 billion in net bookings across the whole company. On that call, Zelnick said the company wanted to avoid claiming success before it happened, The Motley Fool reported. The launch figures followed on 20 November, eleven days after that earnings release.
