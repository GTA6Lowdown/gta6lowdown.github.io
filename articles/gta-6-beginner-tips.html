GTA 6 Beginner Tips: 10 Things to Know First

GUIDEBy GTA 6 Lowdown · Written 22 Nov 2026 · Light spoilers for early mission names only

The short answer

Protect your cash, make your own saves, and slow down. GTA 6 punishes a few GTA 5 habits: you lose the money you're carrying if you die or get arrested, there's no mission replay, and you can only save outside missions. The minimap and Focus also tell you a lot more than they used to.

  • Money: it's cash only at first. Stash it, then bank it once you can.
  • Saves: 15 manual slots, outside missions only (not in the prologue), and one autosave that overwrites itself.
  • Exploring: the map and minimap show which buildings you can enter and where the hidden rooms are.

How we did this: every tip is checked against a named source, mainly VGC's, GTA Boom's and KeenGamer's launch guides, with Red Bull's as a second check, plus what Rockstar North's Rob Nelson told IGN and Famitsu. The mission names mentioned are early ones, as named in those guides, with no story details.

1. Don't carry all your cash

VGC's beginner guide says you lose the cash you have on you if you die or get arrested, and Nelson warned Famitsu that carried cash is at risk. Early on you're cash only, and VGC says bank accounts unlock with a mission called "Buck Me". Until then, VGC points to the loose floorboards in Jason's bedroom as a hiding place for money. There are some photos in there too, which fill in a bit of Jason's past.

2. Save manually, and save often

This is the big one. GTA 6 has no mission replay menu, so once a story mission is done, you can't pick it from a list and play it again, GTA Boom reports. You get 15 manual save slots from the pause menu, KeenGamer says, and you can only save outside missions. Manual saving is also switched off for the whole prologue. There's also one autosave slot, which keeps overwriting itself after missions and some open-world events.

So if there's a mission or a choice you might want to see again, make a manual save before you start it. GTA Boom also says using any cheat turns off saving for the rest of that session.

3. Days are long, and time matters

VGC says day and night last much longer than in earlier GTA games. Some shops close at night, and some missions need a particular time of day. You can check the time on your phone, and sleeping at Jason's house skips the night and lets you change the time of day.

VGC also says the police respond differently at night, so a crime on quiet streets may be easier to get away with.

4. Read the minimap for hidden rooms

Walk towards the back doors of an interior, and the minimap shows whether there's more space behind them, VGC says. It also shows when a door is locked for good, and hidden rooms and staircases appear on it as you get close.

5. Use Focus on doors

Point Focus at a door before you open it. In VGC's guide, a white door is one you can go through, and a red door is alarmed. Opening a red one is a quick way to get the police involved.

6. Learn the two blues on the map

On the world map, buildings come in two shades of blue. VGC says the darker ones have an entrance or an interior, and the lighter ones can't be entered. Close up, the minimap marks the way in, usually with a white line.

7. Feed the snake

Jason has a pet snake called Dolores. Feeding her gives Focus an early boost, according to VGC. It's easy to miss.

8. Call the mechanic

After a mission called "Just A Scratch", you can phone your mechanic to fix your car wherever you are, in most cases, VGC says. It isn't cheap, but it beats a long drive with a smoking engine. And if you're selling the car, fix it first: in Rockstar's demo for IGN, Nelson said he could repair a stolen car before delivering it, because the car fence gives you less money for a damaged one.

9. Don't drive away in the same car, in the same clothes

The police work from what they know about you, such as what you're driving and what you're wearing. Nelson told IGN that buying and wearing a new outfit removes your Heat, and switching to a car they don't know about helps too. Our police and Heat guide has the details, and the short version is on our tips page.

10. Give it a few hours

The National's round-up says Worthplaying found the early chapters spend a lot of time on everyday life, so it takes a while to get going, and TechRadar's review also found the start slower than GTA 5. If the first hours feel quiet, keep going.

ALSO WORTH KNOWING: reportedly there's no full first-person mode, you can't carry your whole arsenal like in GTA 5, and there are no mission medals. Our missing features guide has the full list.
