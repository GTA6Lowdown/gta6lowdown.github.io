GTA 6 Cars: The Vehicles Rockstar Named, and the Real Cars Behind Them

GUIDEBy GTA 6 Lowdown · Written 22 Nov 2026

The short answer

Rockstar has named only a handful of GTA 6 vehicles itself, on its editions page and in its screenshot captions: the '95 Grotti Cheetah, the '67 Vapid Dominator Buggy, the Ganado, the Shitzu Squalo boat, the Dinka Enduro motorcycle, the Crest Kayak and, in the pre-order pack, the '55 Vapid Stanier. Rockstar hasn't published a full vehicle list or a total, as far as we can find, so any "GTA 6 has 500 cars" number is somebody's own count. And as always in GTA, the brands are made up.

  • Named by Rockstar: seven vehicles, plus a Classic Car Collection with no individual cars named.
  • Back from Vice City: the Cheetah and the Squalo, plus an Infernus Nelson spotted in IGN's demo.
  • Not firsts: jet skis, seaplanes and quad bikes have all been in GTA before.
  • Stealing cars: harder than before, with tools, trackers and fences.

Why are there no real car brands in GTA 6?

Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick said in 2022 that real brands have to be iconic and fit the game's world, VGC reported, and NME reports that all of GTA 6's brands are made up. Top Gear Netherlands, citing racing-game developers, says carmakers put conditions on how much damage their cars can show and rarely want them linked to crime. Ferrari once took Take-Two to court in France over a GTA IV car, and in 2014 France's top court rejected its copyright and design claims, but overturned the ruling on its unfair-competition claim, so that part went back for a new appeal.

So GTA has its own makers. Grotti, for example, is GTA's Ferrari, according to fan wikis.

Which Vice City cars are back in GTA 6?

  • The Cheetah. The name goes back to GTA III, and Vice City's Cheetah already looked like a Testarossa, so the '95 Cheetah looks like a deliberate callback, though Rockstar hasn't said so. Grand Theft Wiki also ties Vice City's police Cheetah to Sonny Crockett's car in Miami Vice.
  • The Squalo. It first appeared in Vice City (2002), according to Grand Theft Wiki.
  • The Infernus. In Rockstar's demo for IGN, Rob Nelson spotted what he said looked like an Infernus in a police impound lot. That name goes back to GTA III too, according to GTABase.

What are GTA 6's police cars?

Rockstar hasn't named them, and outlets read different footage differently. Jalopnik reads the Extended Look's main cruiser as Charger-based, while The Drive, writing about Trailer 1, sees Ford Explorer-, Taurus- and Crown Victoria-style cars. Top Gear Philippines compared the Extended Look's pursuit cars to the Ford F-150 and Dodge Charger. A Trailer 1 police car is marked Leonard County, Miami New Times noticed, but we haven't found Rockstar text naming the police force, though the West Coast Customs Tempesta below carried Vice Beach Police Department livery.

Donks and mod shops: Florida car culture

GTA 6 leans into real Florida car culture. Rockstar describes the Ultimate Edition's Rideout Customs mod shop as offering donk stylings, Gfinity reports. A donk is a 1971 to 1976 Chevy Impala or Caprice, WLRN explains, and Miami owners often fit 28-inch rims. The Ultimate Edition's second mod shop, One-Eyed Willie's, is listed on Rockstar's editions page.

How do stealing and driving work in GTA 6?

  • Not every car opens. Nelson told IGN that some parked cars are off limits until you get tools through the story. A key cloner opens some, a slim jim others, and you can always carjack a driver.
  • Scan it first. Your phone has a scanner that shows whether a car is locked, alarmed or tracked, and what it's worth at a Pay n' Spray or a car fence (IGN).
  • Stolen cars aren't yours. Kotaku, relaying Famitsu, reports they vanish when you reboot the game unless you register them, and garage space is limited.
  • Trackers. In IGN's demo, a car with an advanced tracker would ping the police if Nelson drove it slowly, so he had to drive fast.
  • Sell it in one piece. In the same demo, Nelson said he could repair the car first, because the fence would pay less for a damaged one.
  • Busier, tighter streets. Nelson told IGN the streets are narrower and more realistic in scale, and with the traffic density, driving takes a bit of skill.
  • Fuel. Every vehicle has a fuel gauge, according to Edge's preview as reported by GTA Boom.
  • Getting around. There's a ride-share service, Ryde Me, which Nelson called a good way to fast travel. Phil Hooker, head of development, told Edge he's "obsessed with making sure they always turn up", PC Gamer reports.

Real GTA 6 cars on Miami streets

Before launch, Rockstar teamed up with West Coast Customs to build six real-world versions of GTA 6 cars, which were filmed driving around Miami, Complex reported. Wallpaper lists them as the Cheetah, the Coquette, the Manana, the Buccaneer, the Pegassi Tempesta and the Progen Emerus. Interesting Engineering says the Tempesta wore Vice Beach Police Department livery.
