GTA 6 Reviews: What Critics Said (and What They Didn't Like)

GUIDEBy GTA 6 Lowdown · Written 22 Nov 2026

The short answer

Critics loved it. GTA 6 launched with a Metacritic score of 99, which ties it with The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the highest Metascore any game has had. IGN, GameSpot and Game Informer all gave it 10/10. Even so, reviewers had complaints, including awkward controls, clunky combat, missions that tell you exactly what to do, a slow start and a 30fps frame rate.

  • Metacritic: 99 when reviews went up on 18 November, the day before launch.
  • OpenCritic: a Top Critic Average of 99, with 99% of critics recommending it.
  • Best ever: tied with Ocarina of Time on 99, and Rockstar's best-reviewed game. GTA IV had 98, and GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2 had 97.
  • The Game Awards: eight nominations, the most of any game this year.

How we did this: scores come from Metacritic and OpenCritic as reported in launch week, plus the reviews themselves and round-ups that quote them. Review counts keep growing and differ between platforms, so treat any count as a snapshot.

What is GTA 6's Metacritic score?

99. That's where it landed when the review embargo lifted on 18 November, the day before launch, and Push Square reports it held at 99 past 160 reviews. That ties the original Ocarina of Time from 1998, though Push Square points out Ocarina's score comes from just 22 reviews, and that there are simply more gaming outlets now.

What did critics love about GTA 6?

  • The sheer scale and detail. IGN's Luke Reilly called it the single most impressive video game he has ever played.
  • Jason and Lucia. The Washington Post singled out their relationship, according to The National's round-up. Manni L. Perez, who plays Lucia, is nominated for Best Performance at The Game Awards.
  • The people of Leonida. The National's round-up says the Post's review also praises how Leonida's inhabitants behave.

What didn't critics like?

Even the 10s had notes. These are the main ones, from The National's round-up of the reviews:

  • Controls and combat. The Guardian's Keza MacDonald describes pressing the wrong buttons by accident and missions failing for reasons that weren't clear. The Verge found combat clunky and the weapon wheel frustrating.
  • Strict missions. Game Informer says some missions are prescriptive about where you go and what you do, though that keeps the action moving.
  • A slow start. Worthplaying says the early chapters spend a lot of time on everyday life, so it takes a while to get going.
  • 30fps. TechRadar says the 30 frames per second target may disappoint players who want something smoother.
  • No mission replay. IGN couldn't find a way to replay finished missions. See our guide to what's missing at launch.
  • Motorbikes. IGN found bikes stiffer to handle than in earlier games.
  • Some jokes. Ars Technica found some of the jokes stale and Jason the less compelling lead.

Is GTA 6 up for Game of the Year?

Yes. The Game Awards announced its nominees on 17 November, and GTA 6 has eight, including Game of the Year, Best Narrative and Best Score and Music. That's the most of any game this year, but not a record: Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 holds that with twelve, Wccftech points out. The ceremony is on 10 December in Los Angeles.
